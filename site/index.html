What you configure, and what you can change afterwards.

Get the configuration right before you upload

A document is read once, at the moment you file it, against the configuration live at that moment. The list of facts sought and the wording that defines them are fixed for that document. Everything downstream is not.

Editing a draft. Revision 7 stays in use until you publish.

One fact, read three ways

Define a fact once. A credit memorandum, a KYC memorandum and a lender memorandum each read it differently. You do not define it three times and you are not charged three times.

Rearrange without re-reading

Add a section, retitle it, reorder it, bind a different set of facts to it. The change reaches memoranda built from documents filed months ago, at no charge.

A memorandum names the revision that wrote it

Two memoranda generated a fortnight apart under different revisions are not the same document, and they say so. A revision is an integer on an append-only series and cannot change, which is why an old memorandum still reproduces.

What happens to a file you upload.

  1. Upload

    Drop a folder of scans, statements and correspondence.

  2. Classify

    The file is split into parts and each part proposed as a document type.

  3. Review

    You see the parts and the price before anything is filed.

  4. Extract

    Facts are read against your live revision and pinned to it.

  5. Compose

    Sections render the facts bound to them, and nothing else.

Nothing is charged before the review step. A part with no text layer is blocked, not billed — you are told to OCR it and resubmit.
